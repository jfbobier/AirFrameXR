# AirFrameXR 1.5.0: frame generation simplified, every eye at its own pose

Frame generation now has two rates, **x2** and **x3**, and two ways to draw the generated frames, **neural** (the default since 1.4.2) and
**edge** (the GPU's optical flow, the fallback). Everything else was removed: fewer settings, less code, and only the combinations that were
measured to be the best. How it works: `docs/FRAME_GENERATION.md`; the neural engine: `docs/NEURAL_RIFE_CUDA.md`.

The OpenXR engine now follows the standard more closely (new since the 1.5.0 pre-release): each eye is shown at the pose it was rendered
with, a frame without a new picture keeps that picture's timestamp, and repeated frames are recognised from the runtime's own records instead
of the head pose. The streamer also warns when NVIDIA Smooth Motion was active in a game. The design and the measurements:
`docs/PLAN_UNIVERSAL_OPENXR.md` (sections 6 and 6b).

## The OpenXR engine (new since 1.5.0 pre-release 1)

**Each eye at its own pose.** OpenXR gives each eye of a frame its own pose; the streamer used to send both eyes under the left eye's. Mods
that render the eyes one after the other (alternate-eye rendering, as in Luke Ross's mods or NoMoreFlat) submit the two eyes with different
head poses: the older eye is now turned to the newer eye's head pose before encoding, so each eye is shown where it was rendered. In a normal
game both eyes share one head pose and nothing changes. On the author's test builds of this engine, NoMoreFlat's `VR_FrameTimeWarp=1` in
Assassin's Creed IV Black Flag works (with DLSS); before, it ran slower than full rendering.

**A frame without a new picture keeps its timestamp.** When a game submits a frame without drawing a new picture, the frame now leaves with
the timestamp of the picture it shows. Before, it took the new head pose and the headset turned the old picture as if it were new. Lab (a mod
mimic whose frames carry stale pictures): pictures shown under another pose 5.6 % before, 0.0 % now.

**Repeated frames, from the runtime's records.** Our VDXR fork tells the streamer which frames carry a new picture. The *Maximum OpenXR
compatibility mode* (Advanced, off by default as before) used to guess repeats from the head pose and drop them: with the OFXR frame
generation layer it took the generated frames for repeats (lab: 71 new frames a second reached the headset instead of 88). Now nothing is
dropped. For a game that submits each picture two or three times the mode lets it wait once per picture, so it keeps its full rate (lab mimic:
45 pictures a second, 22.5 with the mode off); with OFXR and with normal games it does nothing (lab: 87.7-87.8 new frames a second with OFXR,
mode on or off). It stays off by default because off is OpenXR's letter: the runtime "should" pace every frame wait.

**NVIDIA Smooth Motion warning.** NVIDIA's frame generation for flat games (NVIDIA App > Graphics > *Smooth Motion*), left on for a game,
breaks VR mods: the mod's frames come twice and are later presented from the driver's own thread, which shows as stutter and one eye lagging.
When it was active in a game, the streamer says so in red at the bottom of its window after the game, with how to turn it off; the run log
has a WARN line. It is checked once when the game opens its VR session and once at its first frame, at no cost while playing.

**The setup wizard checks frame generation on your PC.** Its Littlest Tokyo scene test now runs 30 seconds more with frame generation x2
switched on, and the Result page shows what it kept of the refresh rate and what it added to the latency. If the stream does not keep 95 % of the
headset's rate with the neural synthesis, the wizard sets **edge** for you (Advanced, Frame generation, to change it); with your existing
settings it changes nothing. In the lab an RTX 3080 Ti Laptop GPU streamed 65 of 90 fps with neural at a 1760 x 1600 eye (7.7 ms per neural
run; 1.2-1.8 ms on an RTX 5090) and 89 fps at 1248 x 1120 (5.2 ms); edge held 90 at both. Running the network smaller to save time was measured
and rejected: at 0.36 of the eye neural keeps less than half its advantage over edge, at 0.25 it is worse than edge.

**Smaller things:** the streamer's waiting message says "Open the headset app"; the wizard's latency row reads "Total latency (measured by
the streamer)".

## Frame generation (since 1.4.2, as in the pre-release)

**x1.5 is gone; x2 and x3 remain.** At x1.5 the game ran at two thirds of the headset's rate and every frame the headset got was generated at
a different moment between two game frames. The neural network (RIFE 4.4) only places the picture accurately at fixed moments (the middle,
a third, two thirds): close to a game frame it got the picture wrong, and in the author's Hellraiser session the view wobbled at x1.5 while
x2 and x3 were clean. A fix for that made x1.5 stable but no better than the older generation, and x2 looked better. x2 and x3 use only the
moments the network handles well. A setting saved as x1.5 (or the config-only "uncapped" mode) now runs at x2.

**Neural or edge.** In Advanced (Frame generation, *Generated-frame synthesis*): **neural** (default) or **edge**, the synthesis from the GPU's
optical flow, as a fallback; edge also draws every frame on GPUs before the RTX 30 series or without the network's files. The *classic* and
*edge-blend* syntheses were removed: edge had beaten classic (+2.0 dB in the lab judge, sharper at moving edges in 63 of 63 cases, visibly
better on the headset) and edge-blend was indistinguishable from edge. A setting saved as either draws with edge.

**Removed with them:** the Uncapped option, the lag controller and statistics of the 1.5x / uncapped planner (the 10-second
`frame generation perf` line is shorter), and the lab scenarios of those modes. The game fps lock stays and works in every mode.

## What ran, and what did not

**Headset:** the author played Hellraiser on 1.4.2's neural engine at x2 ("works incredibly well") and found the x1.5 wobble that led here.
This build (the author, 2026-11-23): Gears of War E-Day at x3 as before, Hellraiser at x2 at 49 fps rendered and 99 streamed with 0.6 % of the headset's ticks without a new frame.

The OpenXR engine: the author played test builds of it on 2026-11-23/24 (RTX 5090, Vision Pro): Dead Space, Mortal Shell 2, Titanfall 2 VR and
Black Flag with NoMoreFlat (including `VR_FrameTimeWarp=1`), no regression seen. Those builds still dropped repeated frames and one had an extra
rule for a stale eye, both removed since; **this exact build was not played on a headset**.

**Lab, RTX 3080 Ti Laptop, this build (2026-11-24):**
- OFXR, the real layer: 87.7-87.8 new frames a second with the compatibility mode on, the same as off (the pre-release's rules: 71.4, and 2,415
  generated frames dropped); the game at 42.5 fps either way.
- The frame-identity matrix 13 / 13, the mod mimic 7 / 7 (45 instead of 22.5 pictures a second with the mode on, nothing dropped).
- The VR guard with a stand-in module named `NvPresent64.dll`; the streamer's red line through its scripted-events test.
- The harness suite on this build, stopped before its end to ship the same day: 55 of 94 scenarios pass (39 in the suite run, 16 earlier the
  same day on the same engine build). 4 failed: the three neural frame generation rows, which this laptop GPU does not hold at the lab's size
  (they pass against its own measured baseline, see the wizard paragraph above), and one check of the host's clock jitter (12.7 ms against
  12.5; about 2 % of past runs). Not run yet: 35 scenarios (the desktop mirror's handover, latency and screenshot rows, probe variants, the QP
  map, the compiled session, the pacing lab) and the GUI harnesses.
- Not tested: NVIDIA's real Smooth Motion (does the driver load `NvPresent64.dll` into a game that does not use it?), a headset session on
  this build, and **the wizard's new frame generation part, which is compile-checked only** (if it misbehaves, the scene test is marked failed
  and the wizard goes on with defaults).

1.5's frame generation on the RTX 5090 (2026-11-22/23): see the 1.5.0 entry of `docs/progress.md`.
