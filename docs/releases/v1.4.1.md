# AirFrameXR 1.4.1: the streamer owns the field of view, and frame generation follows stick turns

The first 1.4 release (1.4.0 and 1.4.1 were pre-releases). On the Vision Pro the streamer decides the field of view end to end: you pick a picture preset and a field-of-view profile, the streamer sizes the stream and the game for it, tells the headset app the box to show, and spends almost no bits on what your lenses hide. Frame generation now handles stick turns the way it always handled head turns. How it works: `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` (Field of view in 1.4), `docs/FRAME_GENERATION.md`.

**The headset app matters.** The box sent to the app and the sharpening need the visionOS app 1.4 (TestFlight, internal testers). With an older app (1.3.x) the streamer still works: it sizes the stream from the box the app declares and warns when that box is not the one it compiled for. Meta Quest and other headsets keep 1.3's field-of-view behaviour.

## What is new since 1.3.10

**Frame generation: stick turns.** A head turn has always been removed before the generator's motion search; a turn made with the right stick was not, so fast stick turns came out with trailing and double images (unchanged since generation first shipped; a slower game and the sharper 1.4 picture made it easier to see). The generator now measures the camera's turn between two game frames from the frames themselves, removes it before the motion search like the head's, and shows each generated frame at its own moment of the turn; the strip at the edge that a turned view uncovers is filled from the older frame instead of black. In the lab, against frames really rendered at the in-between moment, a 180 deg/s stick turn in a 41 fps game went from 20.2 to 31.3 dB with the double images at a head turn's level; turns that start, stop and reverse are handled the same; head turns also gained (the edge fill). On by default; `AIRFRAMEXR_FG_CAM_TURN=0` (environment variable) gives 1.4.0's generation back.

**Field of view (Vision Pro):**
- **Picture presets.** *Performance*, *Balanced* (default) and *Sharp* in the FOV card replace 1.3's render percentage and stream fit. Each preset is a density at the centre of the view; the stream and the game are sized for it over your restriction, and the foveation follows Apple's own map. Balanced draws about 1.3 times 1.3's centre density, Sharp about 1.7 times.
- **The headset app is told its box** at every connection; no field-of-view sliders in the headset any more.
- **Sharpening in the headset app** (FidelityFX CAS on the luma), on by default, switched live from the FOV card.
- **Mask profiles save work.** The line test (numpad 8 2 4 6 for the edges, **7 9 1 3 for the corners**, or an Xbox controller) records the corners you can see. With a profile that has corner lines, the encoder gives the blocks your lenses never show the lowest quality (the bits go to what you see) and frame generation skips them. The shipped *AVP optimized trade-off* profile has the author's corners. Settings' Encoding reads *Foveated from the mask of <profile>* (the default) or *No foveation*.
- **Profiles:** *AVP optimized performance*, *trade-off* and *quality* ship with the app.
- **A game started before the headset app shows its picture** gets the full render (it used to take a stand-in view: a smaller render, likely no stereo).

**Also:**
- **Tray:** `tray.exe` starts the streamer when your paired headset appears on the network (switch in the launcher, below OpenComposite).
- **Calibration with an Xbox controller** for laptops without a numpad.
- **The field-of-view restriction is read when the streamer starts**; the calibration window restarts the streamer around its scene by itself.
- **The OpenXR runtime** is our fork of VirtualDesktop-OpenXR with upstream's latest changes; a game that repeats a frame no longer costs a copy.

## Fixed since the 1.4.1 pre-release

- **A mask profile encoded the whole picture at the lowest quality** (even the idle green frame missed the headset's chroma key) when two corner lines crossed before the edge between them. The outline sent to the encoder is now always a simple convex shape; one that is not is refused.

## What ran, and what did not

**Headset (the author, RTX 5090 + Vision Pro, 2026-11-19 to 21):** Balanced is visibly sharper than 1.3 (22.4 x 24.7 px/deg at the centre against 17.1 x 18.4). Star Wars Galactic Racer with the trade-off profile and its mask: 88.2 fps against 73.7 with 1.4.0's quality box at the same centre density, about 46 % more bits for each visible pixel, the masked corners not seen. Hellraiser with frame generation at 1.5x: head turns and cutscene cameras clean, stick turns much better than before (measured turns confident on 87 % of the game's frame pairs).

**Lab:** on the 3080 Ti laptop (the 1.4.1 foundation: the runtime fork, the mask in the encoder and in frame generation, all 15 field-of-view scenarios, the golden baseline, frame generation) and on the 5090 (the mask compile check 5 of 5 with the outline checks, the frame generation judge with stick turns, starts, stops and reversals, the streamer path with a stick-turning test app, `pace_game_fg23`; `e2e_fg_latency` fails only a per-pixel cost bound that the 5090 is faster than, and `e2e_baseline` its three pacing checks on the 5090, both as before this change).

**Not run:** any Quest session with this build, the camera turn on a GPU other than the 5090, Sharp with frame generation on the headset, a stick turn that also pitches the view (the camera turn follows yaw, which is what VR games turn on the stick), the stability harness.
