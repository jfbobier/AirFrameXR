# AirFrameXR 1.4.1 pre-release 1: the foundation (test build)

The engine work that comes before the universal-OpenXR attempt: the OpenXR runtime (our VDXR fork) tells the streamer what each frame really holds, a mask profile now saves encoder work and frame-generation work, and the calibration tool works with an Xbox controller. Nothing here has been tested on a headset yet: this build is for that.

## What is new

- **Mask profiles save work.** With a profile that has a mask (the shipped *AVP optimized trade-off*, or a fit with corner lines), the encoder spends almost no bits on what the lens hides (blocks entirely outside the visible window get the lowest quality; any block touching it keeps its normal quality), and frame generation no longer computes those pixels. The Encoding choice in Settings now reads *Foveated from the mask of <profile>* (the default) or *No foveation*; on the Vision Pro the six foveation numbers are computed from the mask and shown read-only.
- **Frame generation with a mask:** in the lab (3080 Ti laptop, Balanced, half rate) the generated frame costs 0.54 ms instead of 0.74, and the stream held 80.4 instead of 77.2 fps.
- **Calibration with an Xbox controller** (for laptops without a numpad): the right stick pushes the line in its direction out, the left stick brings it back, the diagonals move the corner lines, A starts and ends an eye. The numpad works as before.
- **The field-of-view restriction is read when the streamer starts.** A change in the FOV card applies at the next streamer start (the card says so). The calibration window restarts the streamer by itself: without the restriction while its scene runs, then again to apply the result.
- **The OpenXR runtime** is our fork of VirtualDesktop-OpenXR with upstream's latest changes; a game that repeats a frame without drawing a new one no longer costs a copy. Performance measured equal to 1.4.0-pre.1 in the lab.

## Headset app 1.4.1 (TestFlight, internal testers)

- **No stand-in view any more.** Before its immersive space rendered (and while the headset was off) the app declared Apple's raw frustum with
  both eyes at one point; a game started in that second took it as the headset's view (smaller render, most likely no stereo; 1.4.0-pre.1's
  rebuild covered it on the streamer side). The app now declares only its real view. It pairs with this streamer; a stock ALVR streamer is not a target.

## Frame generation and the 1.4 picture presets

Generation renders every frame at the stream's full size. Balanced has about twice the pixels of 1.3's sizing and Sharp 3.6 times, and the game renders the same bigger size: a game that kept up with generation in 1.3 may not on Balanced. If generation looks slower than in 1.3, try the *Performance* preset (about 1.3's pixel count).

## Please test this weekend

1. Your usual games through the new runtime, especially mods that repeat frames (AC Black Flag's kind): any new stutter or black frame.
2. With a mask profile and frame generation on: the corners of the view, at the lens edge, while turning the head (a black sliver there means the mask is too tight).
3. Picture quality with *Foveated from the mask* against *No foveation*.
4. The calibration with a restriction in force, with the numpad and with an Xbox controller: the scene must show the whole view, the streamer must come back with the new fit.
5. A change in the FOV card, then a streamer restart: the game gets the new view.

## What ran, and what did not

**Lab (3080 Ti laptop, mock headset, 2026-11-20; per item in `docs/PLAN_1_4_FOUNDATION.md` section 4):** on the final engine build, the golden
baseline, frame generation (with its new rate and cost checks), all 15 field-of-view scenarios, the OpenXR chain through the new runtime
(plain, texture-array, MSAA, composition layers), the mask in the encoder (map equal to the reference, fill), masked frame generation, the
calibration with scripted controller input, and the mod-loop mimic pass; the repeated-frame scenario fails only its pose check, as 1.4.0 does.
The runtime against 1.4.0's: no difference in any metric (3 pairs). The launcher's compile writes the right mask in 5 of 5 cases. On the
1.4.1 source after the bundle: the installed layout (settings read when the streamer starts), a killed game, the desktop mirror's handover to a
game and to a second one, the logging levels, the keyboard fit, a tracking blackout with generation on and a 5 s client freeze pass; two pacing
scenarios fail on old expectations (the host's frame limit counts generated frames; repeated timestamps within October's range).

**Not run:** the headset; the calibration window against a running streamer (its restart around the scene); a real Xbox controller; a 5090;
the headset app 1.4.1 (built in CI, nothing compiled locally); the rest of the lab suite (most pacing and stability scenarios, most launcher checks), stopped for time.
