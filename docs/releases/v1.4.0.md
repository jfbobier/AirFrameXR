# AirFrameXR 1.4.0: choose your picture edges in the headset

Two new tools, drawn in the headset and driven with the arrow keys of the PC keyboard, replace the 30-minute calibration: what you see is what the stream and the game get. The streamer now **tries** to restrict what a game renders, checks that the game obeys, and leaves it alone when it does not. The foveation of the stream follows Apple's own picture geometry. The desktop mirror has a new, calmer control scheme.

**You need the Vision Pro app 1.4.0 (TestFlight) for the picture edges.** With an older app everything keeps working on the whole panel.

## What is new

- **The same setup for every headset.** The wizard and the settings are one UI for the Meta Quest and the Vision Pro, with one exception: the calibration tool below, which exists for the Vision Pro only and is managed per profile. Nobody has to calibrate. The streamer takes the picture the headset itself declares (the whole panel for the Vision Pro, the lens window for the Quest) and everything works. That default is probably larger than what you really see on a Vision Pro (the panel is declared wider than most faces see through it), so it costs some sharpness and some encoding work; calibrating fine-tunes it.
- **Fit the picture to your eyes** (the Vision Pro calibration, about two minutes, optional, kept as a profile you can switch). A blue rectangle in the headset, one eye at a time: push each edge out with the arrow keys until its black bar is gone, one step back, Enter. The rectangle you see is the picture you get (the *picture edges*); the stream is sized for exactly that, a sharper picture for the same stream. A profile can also be typed in or imported, and set per eye, in the profile manager. The 30-minute ray calibration and the fovgrid button are gone.
- **Choose what the game renders** (every headset, in *Advanced video and performance settings*, button *Choose it in the headset*). The same idea for performance: the game's picture appears in the box you shape, black outside it, arrows pull an edge in, Space compares with the whole view, and the numbers show how many pixels the game no longer renders. On a Meta Quest the picture edges are already what its runtime reports (the lens window), so this is the only choice there.
- **Try to apply FOV restriction** (the former *Restrict the field of view* tick; a performance bonus, never a requirement). A game always **starts unrestricted** the first time: it is told the whole view, so it always looks right. The streamer reads what the game actually draws. A game that follows the box it is told (UEVR, native OpenXR games) gets your restriction from its next start and renders fewer pixels. A game that does not obey (a mod that draws its own frustum, or forces a fixed square frame whatever it is told, like the Gravity Rush 2 mod, the Uncharted community mod or Luke Ross's mods) stays unrestricted: it draws everything anyway, and the stream keeps all of its extra pixels. You lose a little performance on the first run of a game and gain it from the second; you never lose picture to a restriction a game ignores. **Games you trust** go on a list, `config\fov_restriction_whitelist.txt` (one entry per line: a mod's DLL name or a game's executable; it starts with `UEVRBackend.dll`, so UEVR games are restricted from their very first run). A trusted game is still checked: if it turns out not to follow the box it goes back to the whole view and stays there. A game that scales its own resolution is left on the whole view until you tick it or trust it.
- **The stream size follows your bitrate (Vision Pro).** One budget, the constant bitrate in Settings (the profile suggests 300 Mbps): the stream is sized so the encoded frame costs the same bits per pixel as the shipped foveated profile did, sharper where Apple's own display can show it. A lower bitrate gives a smaller stream, with less to encode, send and decode: that is the way to trade picture for latency. A size you type in Settings is kept (until you choose picture edges again), and so is a stream fitted to the window. **If you updated: your stream size will change at the next start to follow your bitrate.**
- **Foveation from Apple's picture geometry.** The foveated encoding is computed from your picture edges together with the rasterization map Apple's compositor really uses: detail that is barely visible at the edges is compressed more, and about 17 % fewer pixels are encoded at the whole-panel default. Split encoding stays on.
- **New desktop mirror controls.** The screen is placed in front of you at the start and stays there; it moves only when you press a **grip** (either controller), which brings it back in front of you. Trigger = left click, A / X / B / Y = double click, no right click. The pointer dot is twice the size, a physical mouse and the beam no longer fight (the mouse has the cursor until you press or sweep the beam), and a *? controls* badge shows the commands.

## Under the hood

- The streamer tells the app the picture edges over the control channel at every connection (one text line, ignored by stock clients); the app declares the smaller of its panel and that view. The game's box is a separate, per-eye setting; both sit inside the panel. Details: `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` and `docs/PICTURE_EDGES_DESIGN.md`.
- The headset tools are scenes of the probe, streamed like a game, so neither headset needs more than the app update above.
- The client logs the raw projection matrices (`[proj]` lines in `logs\alvr.log`) once per declaration: they settle whether up and down are swapped in the declared box.

## Fixed

- The desktop mirror's shader did not compile in the commit that added the controls badge (`pass` is a reserved word in HLSL); fixed before this release.

## What was tested, and what was not

- **tested-pass** (the 3080 Ti builder, mock headset): the whole `e2e_fov_*` family (29 scenarios: the restriction, the picture-edges message, the intake verdicts for fake games that follow, ignore or lie about the box, the fit and restriction tools with scripted keys), the OpenXR SDK's own sample app `hello_xr` as an independent game, the foveation geometry against its oracle (345 of 345 cases), the UI contract and every window at 1080p to 4K, and the mirror's picture for seven desktop shapes in SDR and HDR.
- **tested-pass** (Linux): the visionOS app's stream-transition scenarios (16), including the new picture-edges message.
- **Built, tests being run**: the restriction policy above (the whole view at the first start, the restriction from the next start for games that obey, the whitelist) and the rename of its tick: the policy's logic passes its unit tests and the new `e2e_fov_policy_*` scenarios are written; see `docs/PICTURE_EDGES_DESIGN.md`, section 7.
- **To confirm on a headset**: the whole chain (the tools, the stream size and foveation from your fit, the mirror's controls); which way up and down are declared (`[proj]` lines); the Quest client is unchanged.
- **Not modelled**: a game that ignores the rotated (canted) eye poses; a game that lies about its box (undetectable; the per-game list in the settings is the remedy).
