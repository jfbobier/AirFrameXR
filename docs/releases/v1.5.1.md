# AirFrameXR 1.5.1: about 5 ms less latency on the Vision Pro with frame generation

With frame generation on, a decoded frame used to wait in the Vision Pro for about 10 ms before it was shown, a whole display period.
The cause was found in a measured campaign on the author's RTX 5090 and Vision Pro (2026-11-26, four sessions with the settings
changing every 40 seconds while playing, `docs/PLAN_E2E_FRAME_TIMING.md` sections 10.2 and 10.3): a second frame stood in the headset's
queue behind the one being shown, for 40 to 61 % of the frames, and the streamer's phase lock could neither see it nor remove it. 1.5.1
fixes it on both sides. **It needs the new visionOS client** (TestFlight, see below): with an older client the streamer behaves as before
(measured: no gain and no regression).

## What changed

**The headset shows the frame meant for its tick** (visionOS client, TestFlight). When two frames are waiting, it shows the newest one whose
display moment has come and drops the older one, which would otherwise have pushed every later frame back by a period. A frame that is
alone is always shown; nothing is held back.

**The phase lock, rewritten.** The streamer slides its display clock so that frames arrive just in time for the headset's tick. It used to
fold the headset's wait into one period, which made its target unreachable at 100 Hz: it slid the clock earlier for whole sessions and
corrected 30 times a minute. It now steers the wait as it is (its 90th percentile, at 8 ms), leaving out frames that stood behind
another. Together with the display clock below it settles: about 10 corrections a minute and no drift.

**The display clock.** The streamer's clock ran at the nominal period (10.0 ms at 100 Hz) while the headset's display runs at its own (9.995
to 10.003 ms measured) and the two clocks differ by tens of ppm. The streamer now measures the headset's display period from its tracking
timestamps and runs at it, from about 50 seconds after the headset connects.

## Measured (5090, Vision Pro at 100 Hz, 275 Mbps; the sum of the stages from the game to the display, means)

| Game | 1.5.0 | 1.5.1 | Frames dropped by the headset |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gears of War: E-Day, frame generation x3 (33 to 99 fps) | 63.2 ms | **58.6 ms** (−4.6) | 15 per 1000 (1.5.0: 8) |
| Dead Space, x2 (50 to 100 fps), with the lock at 6 ms | 62.4 ms | **56.3 ms** (−6.1) | 35 per 1000 (1.5.0: 10) |
| AC Black Flag, no frame generation (63 fps) | 70.8 ms | 70.6 ms | no change |

- The gain is the decoded frame's wait, 9.9 ms down to 5.0 ms; nothing else moves (Apple's 20.5 ms from submit to display included).
- A game below the display rate without frame generation gains nothing: no frame ever waits behind another there.
- The cost is a dropped frame now and then instead of a delayed stream: about 1.5 a second at 100 Hz. The author saw no stutter in any of the
  three sessions with the new pick. The lock's 8 ms (against 6 in an earlier session) drops a third fewer frames for 1 ms of latency.
- ALVR's own "total latency" figure reads 57 against 67 ms in these sessions. That is a median of values one period apart and overstates
  the gain: the means of the stages above are the measurement.

## The visionOS client

TestFlight **build 99** of version 1.4.1 with the target pick (internal testers first) (same protocol: any 1.4.1 build works with any 1.5.x streamer). The per-second
`va_slack` line of the experiment build (98) is gone; the 10-second `va_ticks` line stays (the streamer's "headset repeated" figure).

## The Quest

The new lock and the display clock run for every headset. The Quest's own client already limits its queue (ALVR's buffering limit drops
the oldest frame when it holds more than 1.5 on average), and on the Quest 3 the tracking comes at 270 Hz for a 90 Hz panel, so the
display clock finds no period there and the clock stays nominal. Not tested on a Quest at the time of writing.

## Also

- **Two downloads:** `AirFrameXR-1.5.1-streamer.zip` (the streamer) and `AirFrameXR-1.5.1-blackbox.zip` (the Black Box performance
  recorder alone; unzip it into the streamer's folder, or anywhere).
- **Diagnose:** the *Arrival margin* suggestion is removed; it was computed for the old lock's statistic. A margin it saved
  (`video.phase_margin_ms`) is no longer read.
- **Fix:** after the headset renegotiated its rate (the Vision Pro's 90 to 100 Hz at connect), the streamer's tracking thread kept the old
  nominal rate and treated 100 Hz as off nominal.
- **Build:** the streamer is compiled with whole-program optimisation (link-time optimisation across all its crates, one code generation
  unit). No change in speed was measured or is claimed: its CPU work per frame is small.

## Tested

- Streamer unit tests 48 of 48 on the 5090, 8 of them new (the lock's statistic, the display clock with simulated clocks, jitter, stalls,
  lost samples and a 270 Hz tracker).
- The visionOS client's Linux protocol harness (the real `EventHandler.swift`): 41 of 41 scenarios, among them the new `target_pick` (bursty
  arrivals: the pick dropped 38 late frames in 10 s, the picture never stuck).
- The launcher: the Advanced window at 9 screen sizes and scales (0 fail), the Diagnose window's self-test 16 of 16.
- The lab on the 5090 (mock headset, the golden baseline for 110 s): every check passes but the three pacing checks that have failed there
  since 1.3.5, at the same values (client p99 22.2 ms, 1.7 % late, intake p99 19.8 ms). The mock tracks at 283 Hz, so the display clock
  stays out there (the Quest 3 case: the grid stays nominal), and it reports no wait, so the lock keeps correcting in the lab, as 1.5.0's did.
- The display clock on the field's recorded head samples (the 5090 and the Vision Pro, 15 minutes): the first estimate after 49 s,
  10.00224 ms, the same as the experiment build's code.
- The same algorithms on the headset: the campaign's experiment build (identical numbers and rules), three sessions, results above.
- **Not tested:** this exact build on a headset (the release build was not played before publishing), the Quest, 90 and 96 Hz, a worse Wi-Fi
  than the author's.
