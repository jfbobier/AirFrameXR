# AirFrameXR 1.3.5: one field of view choice, a one-minute calibration, a milder foveation

The engine is [1.3.4](v1.3.4.md)'s (the host differs by its version number). 1.3.5 is the field-of-view interface explored on the 1.4 branch, on the proven engine: what 1.4 added to the engine (a headset view sent to the client, a per-game check that a game follows the box, a per-eye restriction) is not in it, and a game shows the same picture in both eyes as before. **Every release now ships the Black Box**: the zip is the bundle (the stock package with the Black Box nested in `blackbox\`, started by `blackbox_perf.exe`), as in 1.3.1.

## What is new

- **One choice for every headset**, in *Advanced video and performance settings > Field of view* (it is set once and forgotten, so it is not on the launcher any more): *Stock vendor view (Apple, Meta)*, the default, or *FOV restriction* with a profile. *AVP optimized* (a window measured with rays on a Vision Pro) is always in the list, even before a headset has connected. The panel rereads its settings whenever its window is activated.
- **The sharper stream, as in 1.3.4.** The Vision Pro profile recommends 0.8 for the headset app's **FoV Scale** slider; the panel says so, and *Fit the stream to the headset's window* then keeps the pixel density over the smaller box. The visionOS client 1.3.5 has the slider again (0.70 to 1.00).
- **A one-minute calibration in the headset** (in *Manage profiles and calibrate...*): four bright lines mark the edges of the picture, one eye at a time; push each outward until the lens cuts it, bring it back until it is clearly visible, Enter ends the eye. It starts on AVP optimized (a Vision Pro) or at 85 % of what the headset declares, and never goes past it. The result is one box for both eyes (the larger value of the two). The 30-minute ray wizard is no longer offered.
- **The setup wizard** preselects the stock view and keeps what you pick; a restriction already in use is kept when it is run again.
- **A foveation that follows your choice (Vision Pro).** Computed from Apple's rate map and the restriction in force, rewritten at each choice, and always milder than ALVR's defaults (a centre at least 10 % larger, an edge ratio at least 20 % lower). The stream size follows your constant bitrate. A Quest keeps the profile's numbers.
- **Passthrough is on by default** for the Vision Pro and the Quest: the wizard's answer now comes after the profile's settings (which turned it off), and the Vision Pro and Meta Quest profiles carry it on.
- **Desktop mirror:** the ray and the mouse appear only for a controller confirmed in use (an input, or an aim that moves away from the head): no ray from the head without VR controllers. The Windows cursor is always drawn as the pointer dot, so a mouse is visible without controllers. Controls: trigger, A / X / B / Y, thumbstick, grip.
- **visionOS client 1.3.5** (TestFlight): the FoV Scale slider is back; the temporary `[ratemap]` log lines are gone.

## What ran, and what did not

On the build PC (RTX 5090): the tool scenarios on a mock headset (`e2e_fov_tool_fit`, `e2e_fov_tool_restrict`) pass; the foveation against a Python oracle (30 cases) and its caps (9) pass; the mirror's picture check passes; the GUIs compile and run; a real session ran on a Vision Pro with End of Abyss (96 fps rendered, 95.8 streamed, the encoded frame at 99.6 % of the bitrate budget). `e2e_baseline` **fails three pacing checks** on that PC (client p99 22 ms against 20, late frames 1.7 % against 1 %, intake p99 20 ms against 16), with the 1.4 harness fix applied; the engine is 1.3.4's, and the likely cause is the lab's mock headset (the phase lock has no 1.4 guard here, so it slides the grid against a mock that never follows): not confirmed. **Not run on a headset:** the line test, the radio choice, the wizard's passthrough default, the mirror's confirmed-controller rule and pointer dot, the client's FoV Scale slider.
