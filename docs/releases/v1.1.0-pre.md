# AirFrameXR 1.1 (pre-release)

Everything in [1.0](v1.0.0.md), plus:

- **A field of view for the game, set from the streamer.** The Advanced card is now *Advanced picture and FOV settings*. A tick, "Override the headset's field of view", opens
  four fields (outer, inner, upper, lower) with an X.XX mask and the angle next to each. Greyed, they show the headset's own field of view as it reported it at its last
  connection; ticked, they start from it and you can only go down (a fifth at most). The game is told the smaller view and a recommended image size reduced in proportion to
  its area, so it renders fewer pixels; the stream keeps covering the headset's whole view (a black margin), so encode, decode, network, frame generation and the headset app
  are unchanged and only the game's GPU work drops. UEVR and OpenXR games read the four angles per eye and the recommended size when they start, so a change applies **at the next
  game start**. Off by default: with the tick off nothing differs from 1.0. The override remembers the field of view of the headset it was made for: on a headset with a different one (a Quest after a Vision Pro) it is not applied and the Advanced window says so; ticking again starts from the new headset's own values.
- **The desktop mirror works left-handed.** The right controller holds the beam as before; a firm press of the left trigger (or grip) hands it to the left controller, a press on the
  right one takes it back (the press that hands it over does not click). A / X, B / Y and both thumbsticks work on either controller. The streamer's settings page and the README
  show real captures.
- **Game audio capture fixed.** A build problem since the 1.0 rename had left the streamer on a device loopback of the PC speakers, whose level follows the system volume; Windows'
  process loopback (independent of the speakers' volume and mute) is active again, and the build now fails when an ALVR patch does not apply instead of printing "ok".
- **The Encoding choice is saved as chosen.** Foveated and "Full frame + split encode" were two separate radio groups: both could be ticked and a foveated choice came back as
  full frame after a restart.
- **A leaner, better explained Advanced window.** Eleven switches that measured as no benefit or a loss are gone and their built-in values stay (send pacing, boundary offset,
  optical-flow scale and preset, the stamp-regime hold, the pick policy, the intake depth, the headset decoder-queue drain, the follow-rate switch, the GPU scheduling class, the
  uncapped content lag; an old `airframexr.json` that still has them is ignored, one log line says so). Every remaining switch explains itself with the numbers measured in the lab
  or on the headset, and a hover has the detail. The HEVC QP map stays on by default (it always was; the window said otherwise and could not turn it off): its measured trade-off is
  written next to it (centre +0.02 to +0.14 dB, edges -0.04 to -0.55 dB at 250 to 40 Mbps, about 5 % fewer bits, no encode-time cost, never judged by eye). The three frame
  generation syntheses stay: edge is visibly better than classic in games (the lab agrees: +2.0 dB whole-frame, sharper in 63 of 63 cases), edge and edge-blend are a tie.
- **Lab tools**: a frame generation judge (`harness/fg_judge.py`, `docs/FRAME_GENERATION_JUDGE.md`: generated frames scored against the frame the scene really has there), the QP map
  A/B (`harness/qpmap_ab.py`), the encoding-radio check, new scenarios for the field of view, the README refresh with `docs/DEVELOPMENT_PHILOSOPHY.md` and the AI-contribution summaries.

Not yet tested on a headset: the field of view override with a real UEVR game and on the Vision Pro, the left-handed mirror, the audio fix on the 5090 (all of it is covered by
lab scenarios on the build PC). IPC version 33: host, shim and package must come from the same build. Frame generation needs an NVIDIA RTX GPU (Turing or later). Use at your own
risk, see the README.
