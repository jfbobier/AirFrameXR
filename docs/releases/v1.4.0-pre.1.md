# AirFrameXR 1.4.0 pre-release 1: the streamer owns the field of view

On the Vision Pro the streamer now decides the field of view end to end: you pick a picture preset, the streamer sizes the stream and the game for it, and the headset app (1.4.0) is told the box to show, so the app no longer has field-of-view sliders. The app also sharpens the picture. A new line test registers the corners you can see (a mask, stored with your profile), and a tray program can start the streamer when your headset appears. How it works: `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` (Field of view in 1.4); why: `docs/FOV_CONTRACT_PLAN.md`.

**The headset app matters.** The box and the sharpening need the visionOS app 1.4.0, which is on TestFlight for internal testers only in this pre-release. With an older app (1.3.x) the streamer still works: it sizes the stream from the box the app declares, warns when that box is not the one it compiled for, and the FOV card shows the slider values that cover your profile. Meta Quest and other headsets keep 1.3's behaviour.

## What is new

- **Picture presets (Vision Pro).** *Performance*, *Balanced* (default) and *Sharp* in the FOV card replace 1.3's render percentage, supersampling, stream fit and "spend the savings on quality". Each preset is a density at the centre of the view, in pixels per degree measured the way Apple's panel spends them (`video.stream_density`); the stream and the game are sized for it over the restriction, and the foveation inside the restriction follows Apple's own map. Balanced draws about 1.3 times the old centre density, Sharp about 1.7 to 1.8 times. The FOV card shows the density and what the game will render.
- **The headset app is told its box.** With a restriction on, the streamer sends the box to the app at every connection (`video.client_box`, an ALVR `Reserved` message; older apps ignore it) and the app declares exactly that. No sliders to set in the headset, nothing to keep in step by hand.
- **Sharpening in the headset app.** AMD FidelityFX contrast-adaptive sharpening on the magnified picture (luma only), on by default, switched live from the FOV card (`video.client_sharpen`).
- **The line test on the numpad, with corners.** 8 2 4 6 move the yellow edge lines (up, down, left, right), **7 9 1 3 move the blue corner lines**; push a line out until it disappears, then Shift + the same key until you just see it. The corners are saved with the profile as a per-eye mask (`mask` in `config\user_fov_profiles.json`, shown on the map and in the FOV card with the share it hides). The calibrate window starts from the profile in use. **The mask is stored only: games are not told it yet.**
- **Shipped profile "AVP optimized trade-off".** The author's fit with per-eye edges and a mask, next to "optimized performance" and "optimized quality" (narrower and taller than performance; 2.50 tangent units squared against 2.22 and 3.39). Shipped profiles may now carry exact tangents, per-eye edges and a mask (`docs/HMD_PROFILES.md`).
- **Tray.** `tray.exe` sits in the notification area and starts the streamer when your paired headset appears on the network; it is idle while a streamer runs. A switch below OpenComposite in the launcher starts it at Windows sign-in (also in the tray's own menu). It matches a headset paired by address too, and it no longer goes blind after an hour.
- **Version 1.4.0** in the windows and the zip.

## Headset app 1.4.0 (TestFlight, internal testers)

- No field-of-view sliders; the app shows the box the streamer sends (or its full view when none is sent).
- The sharpening above.
- **Headset off keeps the stream** (from 1.3.11): take the headset off and the app stays in the background for up to 30 minutes with the game audio going; put it back on and the picture returns. A first build did not get the video back after such a hold; build 81 rebuilds the decoder on resume and reopens the space if nothing renders within 2 s.

## What ran, and what did not

**Headset (the author, 5090 + Vision Pro, release flavour, 2026-11-19):** Balanced with sharpening, "visually much sharper" than 1.3 (22.6 x 24.7 px/deg at the centre against 17.1 x 18.4); the app declared the streamer's box exactly; Star Wars Galactic Racer 73.7 fps (92.9 with 1.3's sizing: the cost of the density is the game's render, about 0.45 ms per game Mpx in that game), Dead Space 96.8 fps over 13.5 minutes, 3.1 % repeated; the audio held through a headset-off hold, the video did not come back (fixed in build 81, **not yet checked on the headset**).

**Lab (5090, mock headset):** the C# session compile equals the reference model for the three presets and 1.3's sizing (`harness/density_check.py` 8 of 8); `e2e_fov_client_box` (the box sent, declared exactly by the headset, the game told the unrestricted view at the stream's size), `e2e_fov_override`, `e2e_fov_restrict_none`, `e2e_fov_tool_fit`, `e2e_fov_tool_mask` (scripted numpad keys move the intended corner of each eye); `fov_profile_check` all checks; `fov_store_check` 27 of 28 (the one failure is known since 1.3.6); UI renders of the FOV card, the launcher and the Advanced switches with no failure; `tray_check` 9 of 9 when the tray was added (not re-run on this build).

**Not run:** Sharp on the headset, sharpening on against off, the corner lines and the trade-off profile in the headset, the hold resume of build 81, any Quest session with this build, `tray_check --expiry` for the one-hour renewal (its setup got no answer from the host), the stability harness. **Known failing in the lab, not caused by this build:** `e2e_openxr_probe`'s three pacing checks (identical with 1.3.10).
