# VisionALVR 0.4.0-alpha

Alpha software, test at your own risk. Streamer for ALVR headsets (Apple Vision Pro, Quest), NVIDIA GPUs with NVENC API 12.2 or later.

## New: frame generation

For games that cannot hold the headset's display rate. The toggle **Frame generation** in the streamer window (and in Settings, `video.frame_gen`)
is off by default and nothing engages by itself. Under it, two ways of running the game and two sources of in-scene motion, all switchable live:

- **Game rate: Half** paces the game at half the display rate (a 20 ms budget at 100 Hz) and the streamer builds the midpoint frame between
  the two newest rendered frames. Predictable cadence, one display period of added latency.
- **Game rate: Uncapped** lets the game render at whatever rate it reaches and synthesises *every* output at the fraction that falls between
  the two newest rendered frames, behind a content lag the streamer sets from the arrivals (or **lag ms** by hand). The **fps cap** holds the
  game a little under its sustained rate: a GPU-bound game otherwise keeps two to three frames in its render queue, which the lag has to cover.
  Read the 10-second `frame generation perf` line in the general log: `app fps` with most `releases late` means the game is GPU-bound and a cap
  at about 90 % of that fps shortens the lag by 20 to 30 ms. Uncapped pays about two game periods of latency by construction; for a game that
  holds exactly half the display rate, Half is the better mode.
- **DLSS motion vectors** (tick, `video.game_motion_vectors`, on by default): when the game runs DLSS and the
  [VisionALVR-OptiScaler](https://github.com/jfbobier/VisionALVR-OptiScaler) DLL sits next to its executable, the generator moves the scene with
  the game's own vectors (exact, no ghosting of fast objects); otherwise it measures the motion with the GPU's optical-flow engine (NVIDIA
  Optical Flow, Turing or later). Install the DLL: download `VisionALVR-OptiScaler-<commit>.dll` from that repository's Releases page, copy it
  next to the game's real executable (Unreal titles: `Binaries\Win64\`) and rename it `dxgi.dll` (or `winmm.dll`, `version.dll`, `dbghelp.dll`,
  `winhttp.dll`, `wininet.dll` when another mod owns `dxgi.dll`). No registry change, no ini. The shim log confirms with `motion vectors:
  attached` and `-> shared copy` lines; the `frame generation perf` line counts `mv` frames. Not compatible with Luke Ross's R.E.A.L. VR mods
  (they hook DLSS themselves) or anti-cheat titles; UEVR games and native DLSS games work.
- Every output, rendered or generated, is stamped with the newest tracking sample and rotated to it: head turns stay as smooth as before.

Measured on an RTX 5090 + Vision Pro at 100 Hz: half rate on a GPU-bound 73 fps game, missed display periods 38 % -> 1 %, total latency 86 -> 57
ms; uncapped keeps a 67-98 fps game at its rate (headset intervals over 15 ms: 1-3 % in every mode); flow 2.3-3 ms + synthesis 0.4 ms (1 ms with
vectors) of GPU time per output. Known limits: the latency above; quad layers are interpolated with the scene.

Design, telemetry and the lab that validates it: docs/FRAME_GENERATION.md, docs/FRAME_GENERATION_UNCAPPED.md, docs/MOTION_VECTORS.md. Next:
a 1/3 rate for 34-49 fps games (half rate's latency, two generated frames per rendered one), then extrapolation from the vectors.

## Also in this release

- Headset-less lab (`harness/simrig`): in-scene motion judge (moving marker, SMOOTH / JUDDER verdict) next to the head-rotation judge, scenarios
  for 50-70 fps and jittery apps with and without the generator, ground-truth vectors from the probe.
- Settings/CLI: `video.frame_gen_rate` half|uncapped, `video.frame_gen_cap_fps`, `video.frame_gen_lag_ms`, `video.game_motion_vectors`;
  `--frame-gen-rate`, `--frame-gen-cap`, `--frame-gen-lag-ms`, `--game-motion-vectors`; live commands of the same names.
- Everything from 0.3 (setup wizard, Find headset, intake gate, foveated Vision Pro profile) unchanged.

## Upgrade

Unzip over the previous folder or next to it; `config\visionalvr.json` and `config\session.json` are kept. The personal zip is not published.
