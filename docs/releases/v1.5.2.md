# AirFrameXR 1.5.2: the Quest gets its own view, a worn controller can calibrate

A hotfix release after the first Meta Quest 3 session with 1.5.1 (2026-11-27). The streaming engine is 1.5.1's (the phase lock, the
display clock, the Vision Pro client's target pick); what changed is setup and settings, mostly for Quest users.

## For Meta Quest users

**The field of view is the Quest's own.** Meta calibrates the Quest's view, and the games render everything it declares. The field-of-view
restriction, its profiles and the calibration are the Apple Vision Pro's: they no longer appear in Advanced or in the setup wizard when a
Quest (or another non-Apple headset) is paired. A restriction you made for a Vision Pro is kept and comes back when the Vision Pro is paired
again; while the Quest is paired it is set aside at every streamer start. Before, a settings file from a Vision Pro reached the Quest: the
Vision Pro's restriction was told to the Quest's games and its mask hid most of the Quest's stream.

**Its own refresh rates.** The Settings window offers the Quest's 72 / 80 / 90 / 120 Hz (it offered the Vision Pro's 90 / 96 / 100), and the
setup wizard starts a Quest at 90 Hz: frame generation x3 of a 30 fps game and x2 of a 45 fps game fit it exactly. 120 Hz stays one click
away; on the author's RTX 5090 it streamed 103 to 105 fps of 120 in Gears of War E-Day and Star Wars Galactic Racer at the full frame.

**The setup wizard pairs a second headset.** With a Vision Pro already paired, pairing a Quest left the wizard waiting at step 2 for the
Vision Pro (the setup files were only rebuilt when the streamer started). The wizard now rebuilds them as soon as the headset is chosen.

## For everybody

**The network test stops in time.** It ramps 100 to 400 Mbps and stops at the first step that does not run clean (the result is the
highest clean step anyway). Before, it pushed on to 500 Mbps with frames that cannot be compressed, which froze a Quest's picture. It goes
past 400 Mbps (450 to 800) only on a cable: the Quest's USB bridge (ADBForwarder, the headset at 127.0.0.1) or a link whose network latency
stays at 3 ms or less at 400 Mbps (Wi-Fi measured 5 to 9 ms), which is how the Vision Pro's Developer Strap shows. A test you cancel now
reads "cancelled", not a failure.

**A worn Xbox controller can calibrate.** Worn sticks rest off their centre (stick drift), and the calibration tool took any stick past
half its travel for a key press: a drifting controller moved the lines by itself. Following the common practice for drift, each stick is
now re-centred on the point where it rests (still for a second, learned again at every rest), then Microsoft's recommended dead zones
apply around it (24 % left, 26.5 % right, rescaled over the travel left). A stick that rests beyond 70 % is ignored, and the tool says to
use the other stick or the numpad.

**The Vision Pro's AVP optimized trade-off is the same for both eyes.** It was the author's own fit, each eye its own; most eyes are
closer to symmetric than one person's. Each edge now takes the larger of the two eyes (the games were already told that), and each mask
corner the more open of the two. A copy of the old profile in your list is replaced by the new one once; if you deleted it, it stays
deleted.

## Tested

On the RTX 3080 Ti laptop lab (mock headset), 2026-11-27, the release build of this version:

- Streamer unit tests 48 of 48 (the release host with whole-program optimisation, the same size as the 5090's build). The visionOS client
  is unchanged: TestFlight build 99 of 1.5.1.
- The launcher: the UI matrix (603 renders at 9 screen sizes and scales, 0 failures), `advice_check`, `fov_profile_check`. The new
  `fov_store_check` lines (the revision refresh, the set-aside for a Quest and back) were added with a stale line fixed, not re-run.
- The lab suite, 79 of 95 scenarios (the run was stopped there): 73 pass, among them the calibration tool's controller scenarios and the
  new `e2e_fov_tool_pad_drift` (a stick resting at 0.58 of its travel). The 4 failures, read against 1.5.0's run on the same laptop:
  - `e2e_fov_restrict` (95 % of the margin samples black, 100 % on 1.5.0): the lit samples are the streamer's green waiting picture before
    the app's first frame (55 such frames in this run, 7 on 1.5.0; the check skips 30). Every app frame has black margins.
  - `e2e_fov_tool_restrict`: one sample of three, 10 frames after a box change, reads part of the margin lit; the tool's boxes are the same
    as on 1.5.0 frame for frame and the other two samples pass. Most likely the same start-up shift moving the frame the sample reads (not
    proven).
  - `e2e_probe_array_resubmit` (`pose_roundtrip`): fails the same way since 1.4 (2026-11-20).
  - `e2e_session_compiled` (eye width 2112 against 2336): its expected size dates from 2026-11-10, before 1.4 sized the stream for a
    density, and it had not run since; the compiled session carries no field-of-view setting, so 1.5.2's changes do not reach it.
  - Blocked: `e2e_fov_from_settings` (the launcher's self-test program was not built in that lab folder), `e2e_mirror_desktop` (the
    laptop could not capture its desktop in that session). Not run: the pacing scenarios and five others.
- **Not tested:** the network test's cable branch (no USB-tethered headset in the lab), a real worn controller (the community's numbers and
  one scripted case), the Quest pages on a Quest after this build, this build on a headset.
