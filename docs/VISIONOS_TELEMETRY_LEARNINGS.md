## How it was collected (telemetry builds 2026-11-08 to 2026-11-10; removed in 1.3.9)

- Client branch `additional_telemetry` (fork `kteque/alvr-visionos`, from `kteque/1.3.6` `099e02c`). Read-only: it writes log lines and changes no state. Compiled in CI and uploaded to TestFlight through the fork's TestFlight workflow; not otherwise verified to leave behaviour unchanged.
- Lines start with `[tele]` and go through `alvr_log` at error level (the only level the streamer logs while streaming), so they appear in the streamer's session log (`alvr.log`). Find them with `grep "\[tele\]"`.
- Line types:
  - `drawable color WxH layers N views N`, `view i viewport WxH at x,y`, `ratemap layer i screen WxH physical WxH` (or `ratemap none`): once per stream start (from build `b7b4e1a`; the first build logged them before the log connection existed, so they never appeared).
  - `decoder hardware Optional(1) codec <fourcc> WxH`: once per decoder session. `Optional(1)` = hardware decoder in use. `1752589105` = `hvc1` (HEVC).
  - `pick over N ticks: fresh / resubmit / reprojected / none, queue depth at pick, wait for a late frame, age of a fresh frame`: every 10 s (commit `61a8f6f`, added to the branch after `b7b4e1a`; not written in the session that wrote this file). Reprojected = the compositor tick had no new frame and the client reprojected the previous one.
  - `display interval mean/min/max ... over N frames`: every 10 s and on a thermal change. From the compositor's presentation times. From `b7b4e1a` it also has `N over 15 ms`, `deadline misses`, `min slack` (end of `endSubmission` against the predicted `renderingDeadline`), `thermal` (0 = nominal) and `stream target` (the rate the stream was set to).
- Earlier probes of the same kind: `[ratemap]` (1.3.2 to 1.3.4, result went into `RateMapFoveation.Milder`), `[proj]` and `[view]` (only on the archived 1.4 line, tag `archive/1.4-experiment`).

## Findings

Settled by measurement (logs below). Device: Apple Vision Pro M2, streamer 20.14.1 session, HEVC, stream target 90 Hz, constant 275 Mbit/s.

1. **The display ran at 100 Hz for the whole session, with the stream target at 90.** 344 ten-second windows over two logs (about 57 minutes), mean interval 10.0 ms in every window; no switch to 90 or 96. The client offers `[100, 96, 90]`. Why the system chose 100 is not in the logs. Hypothesis (from the research notes, not shown by our data): visionOS picks 90/96/100 from the room's lighting (flicker sensor, 50 Hz mains in France), so the app cannot choose it.
2. **Hardware HEVC decoding in use** in both sessions (`decoder hardware Optional(1)`), stream 3840x1664 as encoded.
3. **Thermal state 0 (nominal)** in all 344 windows. Sessions were 36 and 21 minutes, so this says nothing about longer sessions.
4. **Occasional doubled display intervals are client deadline misses.** In the 2026-11-09 midday log, every interval over 15 ms coincides with a submission that ended after the compositor's rendering deadline (48 of 48; min slack -5.8 ms, at 90 Hz = 11.1 ms frames). Earlier logs (night) had the same doubled intervals (~0.2 % of frames) but no deadline counter. Cause of the misses not established.
5. **The display ran at 90 Hz in the midday log (13:00), 100 Hz in the night logs (23:55 to 00:39).** Consistent with the lighting hypothesis in finding 1, but the two sessions also differed in stream settings (see Sessions), so it is not isolated.
6. **Geometry numbers visionOS gave the client** (midday log, one session, fit not recorded): drawable colour texture 2432x2368, 2 layers; each view's viewport 6261x5023 (larger than the texture, so not texture pixels: probably the screen-space size the rate map is defined over; interpretation unconfirmed); rate map `screenSize` read as 0x0 and `physicalSize` 2432x2368 per layer. A 0x0 screen size means the rate map was not usable as read; whether foveation was active is not established.
7. **Repeats track the frames the stream actually delivers, not the pick policy** (midday log, 17 windows; the log's `[STATS]` lines give the streamer's `server_fps` and the client's `client_fps` per second). Per 10 s window, the share of compositor ticks without a fresh frame correlates 0.89 with the share the delivered rate cannot cover, (90 - client_fps) / 90. Windows at client_fps 90 had 0 to 3 % repeats; windows at client_fps 48 to 72 had 22 to 45 %, and `server_fps` in those windows was 50 to 72: the game or the streamer was not producing frames (a loading scene in the first minute). So in this log the repeats are born upstream of the client. Open: two windows repeat clearly more than the delivered rate explains (13:00:55 +3 points; 13:01:05 +13; 13:03:26 +30 with client_fps 65 and 58 % repeats). The client's queue holds 2 frames and discards the oldest on overflow, which would turn bursty arrival into extra repeats; the overflow drops were not counted until build `visionos_client_performance` (`queue overflow drops` line).
8. **The streamer in that log was slow to encode**: `encode_latency_ms` 11 to 17 ms in most windows (above the 10 ms budget of a 100 fps stream), `server_fps` 42 to 109 and 84 to 96 in the steady part, constant 300 Mbit/s at 4096x3609. Which streamer and machine produced it is not recorded in the log; note it before reading it as a statement about the AirFrameXR engine.
9. **Reprojection share varied a lot at a 100 fps stream on a 90 Hz display** (midday log): 15 % of compositor ticks overall, 0 to 58 % per 10 s window, 10 ticks with no frame at all. The windows with the most reprojection do not line up with the windows with the most deadline misses.
10. **The client drops very few frames; the repeats the launcher shows are the stream's, not the headset's** (2026-11-09 evening logs, 16 and 15 minutes at 100 Hz; the lab model agrees). Frames lost by the client: about 0.1 % (queue overflow 0.05 %, missed deadline 0.04 to 0.09 %), the GPU never late (0 of 87,932 command buffers). The tile "Headset shows 84.7 fps, repeated periods 14.9 %" (Dead Space, 76.8 min) counts display ticks without a new picture, `displayed_fps` = display rate x (1 - repeats). A tick is repeated when the stream delivered no new frame for it: a 96.7 fps game on a 100 Hz display forces 3 %, stalls and a free-running game's drift make up the rest (per-window correlation of repeats with the delivered shortfall 0.95 to 1.0, the client adding about 0.3 points). So "repeated" is not "dropped": read it as "the game and stream did not give the display a fresh frame", and look upstream (game fps, `server_fps`, frame generation), not at the client's pick or queue. Earlier readings that blamed the pick policy or counted repeats as client losses are superseded; finding 4 (deadline misses) stays, at 0.04 to 0.09 %.
11. **ALVR's total latency is pose to picture, and its "network" is a remainder.** `total_latency_ms` = the client's measure from the tracking pose to the frame shown. In `[STATS]` (and the tile) `network_latency_ms` is not measured: statistics.rs takes the total and subtracts game time (pose received to frame present), server compositor, encoder, decode, decoder queue, client render and vsync queue; what is left is called network. So the streamer's own share is small (Dead Space night log: encode 6, decode 6, network 7, about 19 ms of a 96 to 97 ms median, p95 107) and the rest is the game's time from the pose to its frame plus the queues. The launcher tile used to show only the total, which read as a streaming latency; it now shows the split (1.3.8). Which part of the rest is the game's render queue and which is the frame-start hold has not been measured on this log (the host's windows carry `game_ms` and `server_compositor_ms`; this log's `[STATS]` lines do not).
12. **The reconstruction filters cost nothing measurable on a stream at or above the screen's resolution.** GPU draw mean 2.22 ms at 100 Hz on an 8192x3680 stream (`alvr(3).log`, 377,192 frames) against 2.23 ms with the hardware formats and no filters on 7104x3200. On a small 3840x1664 stream stretched to the screen it is about 4.0 ms (three logs): the luma filter runs on most pixels there. The GPU was never what missed a frame (4 of 196,388 command buffers late on the small stream). Colour and sharpness judged good on the device by the owner.
13. **The catch-up pick (show the newer of two queued frames) works in the field.** Depth at pick in the roughly 100 fps windows fell from 1.03 to 1.06 (three logs before) to 0.82 to 0.87 (`alvr (6)`, `alvr2 (1)`), the fresh-frame age in the queue from 5.7 to 6.1 ms to 3.3 to 3.6 ms; overflow drops and repeats unchanged. In the lab (Linux harness, display switch with a render stall) the old pick left a standing backlog of about one period (18.6 to 21.4 ms shown-frame age against 8 to 10 ms). **Revised the same night (finding 17): it buys about 0.5 ms of queue age and costs about 1 point of new frames per tick; it was switched off in 1.3.9.**
14. **Deadline misses come from CPU spikes of 3 to 14 ms (up to 65 ms in one window) in single `queryDeviceAnchor` calls, not from the GPU, the lock, the decoder or the walk-back loop.** Render-thread split: `view+tracking` dominates the worst frames, inside it `send_tracking`, and inside that the refetch query, the loop's query and the hands + send; the walk-back loop never retried (0 of 50,942 calls); the frame-queue lock never exceeded 1.4 ms. Skipping the refetch query saved part of it: misses per 1000 frames 2.93 -> 2.19 (p = 0.003) in one 45-minute log, no difference in two others; the first query of a frame absorbs part of the stall whichever call it is. The head velocity the client sends is discarded by the host (zero velocity).
15. **The display rate is not fixed within a session, the host did not follow the switches seen in the field, and 90 Hz windows miss four times as often as 100 Hz ones.** Excursions seen: 2026-11-09 21:16 (100 -> 90 Hz, 10 s, 78 misses), 2026-11-10 00:17 (-> 96 Hz, 15 to 20 s) and five in the afternoon log (to 96 / 93 / 95 / 91 / 94 Hz, two of them long stays at 90 Hz of about 130 s and 50 s). In the two cases with a host log the host logged no `client display rate` change and `server_fps` stayed at 96 to 102; its phase lock sat pinned at -2.00 ms for about 50 s after the 96 Hz switch. The lab said the host re-locks within seconds (`pace_switch_*`), so lab and field disagree; the cause is not found. Misses at 90 Hz: 20 per 1000 frames against 4.6 at 100 Hz (afternoon log) although the budget is larger. Next: plan section C in `docs/VISIONOS_TELEMETRY_LEARNINGS.md`. **Amended 2026-11-10 evening (see the CORRECTION entry): the two excursions of 21:16 and 00:17 were deadline-miss storms on a fixed 100 Hz display, not rate changes, so the host not following them was correct. The afternoon log still has two real stays at 90 Hz (misses 23 per 1000 there against 3.3 on the 100 Hz grid), and whether the host follows a real switch is untested in the field.**
16. **Apple's render pipeline page** (Understanding the visionOS render pipeline): the display driver wakes at a regular interval and takes the compositor's newest image, so a frame that is not ready is not waited for and the previous one is shown (the mechanism behind every doubled interval). Rules for Metal apps: submit a new frame on each compositor update, match the display rate (usually 90 Hz, higher under some environmental conditions), query pose and foveation map right before encoding GPU work, avoid long stalls (the system terminates an app that takes too long to submit; no number given). Nothing on rate switches or locking the rate.
17. **What moves the needle on the pick and the pose fetch (pacing rig 2026-11-10, then a headset A/B the same night): a longer wait for a late frame with the catch-up off, +1.1 points of new frames per tick; nothing else was found.** Field A/B on build 64 (`alvr.log`, 117 clean windows): catch-up off + 8 ms wait against catch-up + 5 ms: new frames per tick 97.30 % against 96.18 % (SE 0.16 and 0.24), repeated ticks -29 %, reprojected 34 -> 7 per 1000, resubmits 4 -> 20 per 1000, queue age +0.46 ms, deadline misses 0.6 -> 1.0 per 1000. The rig had predicted +0.8 points. The refetch query has no effect on the picture or on pose accuracy (diff in new frames +1.0, -0.2, +0.2 points, all inside the noise; prediction error 0.013 to 0.026 deg and 0.12 to 0.19 mm in both modes). Shipped in 1.3.9: pick wait 8 ms, catch-up off, original per-frame pose fetch. See the session entry below.
18. **Translation correction through Apple's compositor was built and tried, and dropped: the owner could not tell the difference, and Klepton, a respected visionOS client, ships without it.** The client's warp corrects rotation only (our shader draws the picture as a quad at 500 m; depth is cleared to 1e-9, effectively infinity; `drawable.deviceAnchor` is the anchor at the display time), so the head's movement since the frame was rendered (about 42 mm at a 70 ms pose age, one window of `alvr-old.log`) is not corrected. Test branch `translation_test` (`kteque/alvr-visionos`, `b8e12cc`, TestFlight 1.3.9 (67)): the anchor queried at the frame's own pose time as `deviceAnchor` (our warp collapses to nothing), the depth cleared to a plane at 1.5 / 3 / 6 / 12 m, a setting to switch it off. Results: at 3 m the frame's anchor was used on 99,672 of 99,706 frames (99.97 %, 100 windows, `alvr (2).log`), pacing unchanged (`client_fps` median 99, no loss, 97.9 fps game, repeated 10.4 % typical); **the owner could not tell the difference at 3 m and could not at 1.5 m either** (no Off comparison on record, the 1.5 m client log was not captured: `alvr (3).log` is byte-identical to `alvr (2).log`). Not tested: 0.3 m or 0.15 m, which would have shown whether the compositor honours our depth at all. Klepton (`glovebox-vr/glovebox`, `klepton/`, commit `521e6a6` of 2026-10-12, read in code): default plane 500 m, "rotation only, deliberately" (`runtime/gfx/kl_reproject.h`, `kl_reproject.c`); it first used 2 m and wrote that this was a misattribution: visionOS applies parallax for a plane at the depth it is handed, "over a degree" at 2 m that the picture does not contain, and applies it on the frames it chooses to correct and not on others, "two images in two places"; far away the system's correction stays rotational, "the only correction that is right for a picture with no per-pixel depth". Its `drawable.deviceAnchor` is the display anchor, like ours in 1.3.9. Its own shader can correct translation for a plane at `KL_REPROJECT_DEPTH` (inert at 500 m, the default); it measured on device (JKXR) that a head yaw translates the eyes sideways and the world swims, a nod much less. `KL_CP_DEPTH=1` (the game's per-pixel depth for the compositor) is an off-by-default prototype and does not apply to us (no depth in the stream). Decision: 1.3.9 stays as shipped (500 m, rotation only, equal to "Off"); `translation_test` is not merged. If translation ever matters, do it in our own shader on every frame for a plane at a chosen distance (deterministic, not left to the compositor), and test with head yaw at a near object. Correction of the survey line that stood here: oxrsys (2 m plane) and VeRantyx (10 m plane, the render pose as anchor) pass a near plane to the compositor; Klepton does not. Other results of the survey (2026-11-10, repos read, nothing run): nothing in other repos beats our pick, queue, decoder (hardware, RealTime or async at most) or QoS handling; upstream PR #168 says `preferredFrameRateRange` "seems to be ignored" (our finding for the 100 Hz request); VeRantyx's adaptive prediction horizon (capped at 60 ms, because longer horizons made ARKit overshoot) conflicts with our fixed design and our pose error is already about 0.02 deg. Not covered: KRVR, Godot, Bevy, Unity, Apple's sample project, issue histories.

## Open questions

- (answered, see Sessions 2026-11-09 evening 2) What is the client's real draw cost? GPU 2.2 ms mean, CPU 1.6 ms mean per frame, on the hardware YCbCr path without filters. This is the baseline for the bicubic filters.

- (answered by the `alvr(1).log` session, see Sessions) How many frames does the real client lose? 0.05 % to the queue cap and 0.04 % to missed deadlines in 16 minutes at 100 Hz.

- (answered: NO, see finding 15) Is the display rate fixed for the whole session? It was in the first six logs; the 2026-11-10 logs show excursions to 96 Hz, to 90 Hz and back, and long stays at 90 Hz.

- Why 100 Hz at night and 90 Hz at midday? Lighting is the hypothesis; stream settings differed too. Test: same session settings in daylight and under artificial light, compare the `display interval` lines.
- Why did the client's `stream target` read 90 Hz when the session asked for 100 fps (midday log)? Not looked into.
- (answered in part, see finding 14) What makes the client miss the rendering deadline? CPU spikes of 3 to 14 ms in single `queryDeviceAnchor` calls in the render thread. Not answered: why 90 Hz windows miss four times as often as 100 Hz ones (finding 15).
- What the 6261x5023 viewport and the 0x0 rate-map screen size mean, and whether foveation was active. Panel resolution is still the estimate (3660x3200 from iFixit, 3648x3144 from Omdia); our default declared view size is a number, not a measurement.
- Does the rate map change with gaze? Not tested; we do not depend on it either way.
- Thermal behaviour over 30 to 60 minutes at the final profile: not tested.
- Decode callback time and dropped-frame counts: not logged (would hook the decode path).

- Does asking visionOS for 100 Hz when the stream is 90 change anything? (1.3.9 does it, a hack; in the 2026-11-10 night sessions the display ran 100 Hz with 90 asked.) The 1.3.9 client has no telemetry, so look at the host's `display_rate` / phase-lock events in `AirFrameXR.log` and at the `[STATS]` `client_fps`; a 90 Hz stay would show as `client_fps` near 90 with the host's period event.
- Why does the headset drop to 90 Hz in some stays (the afternoon log: 15:57 for 130 s, 16:13 for 50 s)? Not the client's request (it asked 90 and got 100 in the night sessions); load, thermal state and the compositor's own rule are untested.
- Dead Space: the owner saw the pose shift a little. The refetch and the display-rate follow are excluded (pose prediction error is the same in both refetch modes, the rate request does not touch the pose). Candidate not excluded: the warp corrects rotation only and the translation gap at a 70 ms pose age is about 40 mm, which shifts near objects when the head moves; the game's mod is the other candidate. The translation test (finding 18) did not change what the owner saw: unexplained, and no longer a candidate we pursue.

## Background (not measured by us)

From the research notes of 2026-11-08, secondary sources, treat as leads: Apple publishes no panel resolution or FOV; visionOS supplies projection, viewport and rasterization rate map per drawable; the M2 media engine lists H.264/HEVC/ProRes, not AV1; Wi-Fi is 802.11ax, 5 GHz, 80 MHz, 2 streams (1.2 Gbit/s PHY, application throughput unpublished); the compositor expects extended linear Display P3. ### C.7 Outcome (2026-11-10)
Executed on the builder (rig client in Arch WSL2, real engine, simulated display and network; details in `docs/VISIONOS_TELEMETRY_LEARNINGS.md`, session "the pacing rig..."). Result: the display never switched in the field except for two real 90 Hz stays; the other "switches" were deadline-miss storms, so C.2 (the host not following) has no field case behind it. Candidates tried: period refinement (dead end), phase margin 3 ms (calmer lock, latency effect unexplained, headset decision), pick wait and catch-up (a longer wait with catch-up off gives +1.1 points of new frames, confirmed on the headset), the refetch (no effect). ### A. Fewer blocking tracking queries (deadline misses, 0.2 % of frames)

Evidence: the render-thread spikes (3 to 14 ms) sit in single `queryDeviceAnchor` calls; the walk-back loop never retries (0 of 50,942 calls). Three queries per frame: the refetch and the loop query in `WorldTracker.sendTracking`, one in `Renderer` after it.

Candidate: remove the refetch (`queryDeviceAnchor(lastHeadTimestamp)`). Its only use is `lastHeadPose`, the base of the head velocity sent to the streamer. Replace by the previous frame's stored pose.

- Pros: one blocking call less per frame; fewer misses if the stall is per call.
- Cons: the gain may be nil if the first query of a frame absorbs the stall; the velocity gets worse (two predictions made 10 ms apart instead of one moment), and the streamer uses it for pose prediction; device anchors have hidden state, so removing a query may change the others; the tracking packet changes.
- Test: a constant, then alternate the variant every 60 s inside one session, each telemetry window printing its mode; compare miss rates (about 100 misses per mode per 20 minutes) and judge head motion by feel. Keep only if misses fall and the motion feels the same. Otherwise look at the `Renderer` query.

The 21:16:44 and 00:17:07 events are storms of deadline misses on a 100 Hz display (minimum interval 9.9 ms in every window; a real 90 Hz session, `alvr (2)`, has 11.11 ms). ### B. Display rate changes (90/96/100 Hz chosen by visionOS)

Input: `deep-research-report (1).md` (2026-11-09, a literature search, not our measurement) and our logs. Agreed: no public API locks the rate (Apple documents the system choosing 90/96/100 Hz from the lighting, with a flicker sensor; `preferredFrameRateRange` is a hint; `AVDisplayCriteria` is for tvOS; no entitlement; private APIs break App Review 2.5.1). The design is already the one it recommends: the compositor clock is the master (`presentationTime`), the host follows the headset's vsync and phase, latest-wins queue, reprojection when no frame, no codec reset on a rate change.

Field fact that makes this worth doing: in `alvr (4).log` the display changed once, 21:16:44 100 -> 90 Hz and 10 s later back. The 90 Hz window had 78 of the session's 353 deadline misses (8.7 % of its frames against 0.2 % normally) and one 80 ms display interval. The 10 s windows cannot show what happened inside.

Steps, in order:
1. **Per-event telemetry (client only, no behaviour change).** On a confirmed rate change, log the 2 s around it frame by frame: display interval, slack, queue depth, picked fresh/repeat, and the arrivals per 100 ms. This tells whether the misses are the transition or the 90 Hz period.
2. **Lab: extend the switch scenarios** with 96 Hz, a rapid 100-90-100 and 100-96, and a stall at the switch; measure the frame age at presentation and the repeats around the switch (the report asks for the age distribution, not the mean fps).
3. **Backlog at a switch (behaviour change, lab first).** When a change is confirmed, drop everything but the newest decoded frame (the report's "drop all but newest"); our queue cap is already 2, oldest dropped, so check in the lab whether it changes anything before touching the device.
4. **Confirmation before reacting.** The report suggests three consistent intervals plus an EWMA period. Our client only logs the rate; the host reads the phase and period from the headset reports (engine side, `phase.rs`): check how it treats one odd interval before changing anything. Engine edits need the owner's go.
5. Optional: the host sending the measured vsync rate next to `fps_target` (engine edit, owner's call).

Not planned: forcing a rate; private APIs; Apple's Foveated Streaming framework (owner: no CloudXR).

Order: B1 can ship in the same build as A (both telemetry or alternating test).


### C. Rig session: real client code + real streamer, honest HMD physics (executed 2026-11-10, outcome in C.7)

Goal: find an end-to-end pacing algorithm (streamer + client) that is more robust at a steady rate, through display-rate transitions and through game hiccups, and first reproduce the open field problem (**superseded: the host did not follow because the display never switched, see the correction above; the open problems are the miss storms and the phase lock's sawtooth**): the host did not follow either real rate switch (90 Hz on 2026-11-09 21:16:44, 96 Hz on 2026-11-10 00:17:07; `server_fps` stayed about 100, no `client display rate` host log line, phase lock pinned at -2.00 ms; the lab said it follows). Not a goal: anything that needs the headset to judge (picture, head feel).

#### C.0 Rig
- **Client side, Linux (WSL2 here, networking already `mirrored`, `eth1` 10.199.42.65/19, behind Wi-Fi):** the Swift protocol harness (`external/alvr-visionos-pr/tools/protocol-harness`: the real `EventHandler.swift` and pick policy, `DisplaySim.swift` as the render thread). ### Apple, "Understanding the visionOS render pipeline" (developer.apple.com, read 2026-11-09 from the documentation JSON feed)
Applies to Metal apps in a Full Space through CompositorServices (ours). What it adds, beyond what we measured:
- The display driver wakes at a regular interval and takes the compositor's newest image (the display pulls; it does not wait for a frame). So a frame that is not ready is not waited for: the previous one is shown. This is the mechanism behind every doubled interval in our logs.
- The pacing rules: submit so that the compositor gets a new frame on each of its updates; keep the Metal frame rate equal to the display refresh rate ("usually 90 Hz, it can be higher under certain environmental conditions", the 100 Hz we see); query a new pose (and foveation map) for each frame immediately before encoding GPU work (our `queryDeviceAnchor` calls sit right before the encode, as asked; the refetch A/B changes only the extra velocity query).
- "Avoid any long frame stalls. If your app takes too long to submit a new frame to the compositor, the system terminates it." No number is given. Our worst observed stall is 80 ms (the 21:16 switch) and the app survived; the harness's long stalls are on the host side, not the render thread.
- Profiling: Metal System Trace and its Display timeline for apps that bypass the render server. Nothing in the page about a rate switch, the flicker sensor or locking the rate.

### 2026-11-10 (evening): CORRECTION, the two "display rate switches" were deadline-miss storms on a 100 Hz display
The client's `DISPLAY RATE CHANGED` line fires when a 10 s window's *mean* interval moves by more than 3 % from the previous window. A burst of doubled intervals does that without any change of rate. The **minimum** interval of the window tells them apart: a true 90 Hz display has a minimum of 11.1 ms, a 100 Hz display with misses keeps 9.9 ms.
- Every window of the logs `alvr` 1 to 6 and `alvr2` (the ones read when this entry was first written): minimum interval 9.78 to 10.04 ms, except `alvr (2).log` (the midday session), which ran at a true 90 Hz for all 17 windows (minimum 11.11 ms). **No mid-session change of display rate in those logs. The afternoon log `alvr(3).log` has two (see the addendum below): real switches do happen.**
- 2026-11-09 21:16:44 (`alvr (4)`): mean 11.129 ms, **min 9.899**, max 80.0, 79 intervals over 15 ms, 78 deadline misses (baseline 2), min slack -64 ms. One 80 ms stall and about 78 doubled intervals on a 100 Hz grid. The next window (21:16:54) is back at baseline.
- 2026-11-10 00:17:07 (`alvr (6)`): mean 10.375 (96.4 Hz), **min 9.887**, max 30, misses 33, then 21, 7 (with a slack of -107.7 ms), 10, 13, 6 in the following windows. A miss storm of about a minute, no stall at its start.
- So the host not following was **correct**: the display period never changed. The statements above in this file that the display "switched" to 90 and 96 Hz, and that the host "did not follow either switch", are superseded by this entry. The streamer's `RateEstimator` is not shown to be wrong; it has not been tested against a real rate change in the field, because none has happened.
- **Phase lock "pinned at -2.00 ms" is the controller's ordinary state, not a reaction to the storms.** `AirFrameXR.log` of the same nights (1906 phase-lock lines, 2026-11-09 20:01 to 2026-11-10 00:21): 16.3 % of the lines sit at the -2.00 ms limit outside the storms, 23.2 % within 75 s of one; the arrivals oscillate between 0 and 5.6 ms before the tick (target 7), a sawtooth of slide, recover, slide. The controller does not reach its target margin in the field. The rig reproduces the same sawtooth.
- Model made from this (rig client, `harness/scenarios/rig_local_*`): display fixed at the session's rate; baseline misses 2.2 per 1000 frames (a render-thread cost over the 4.3 ms slack: `send_tracking` window maxima 4.4 ms, up to 14.4 ms, so 20 ms intervals, 30 ms for the biggest); storms = an optional stall, then a miss rate peak*exp(-t/tau) over the baseline: `30:0.31:2.8:70` reproduces the 21:16 window (72 misses against 78, stall shown as an 80 ms interval) and `30:0.05:15` the 00:17 sequence (34, 19, 17, 12, 6 against 33, 21, 7, 10, 13, 6). Quiet windows: 1 to 4 misses, mean 10.01 to 10.04 ms (field 10.016 to 10.027).
