Frame size at each step on the Vision Pro, FOV restricted and foveated

One eye through the pipeline. The game renders a smaller picture, and the render quality slider can raise it up to the stream's own size. Every stage after composition carries the headset's full frame with a black margin where the game drew nothing.

Where the saving is

Only the game's GPU work drops: 5.6 Mpx per eye instead of 11.4, which is 49%. Encode time, bitrate and decode time stay where they were. The render quality slider spends part of that saving on a finer render, up to 11.4 Mpx.

Why the margin is cheap

The margin lies in the periphery, where the warp shrinks the most. It is 51% of the composed frame but only 34% of the stream frame, and black blocks compress to almost nothing.

Headset side

The client unwarps into the headset's full field of view as before. The margin shows as black at the edges, with the picture placed inside it.

Numbers: the Vision Pro's 3552 × 3200 per-eye frame (the game's recommended size equal to it) and the "recommended" row of docs/AVP_FOV.md, which is the field of view measured with the FoV grid, left eye, plus 3 degrees: outer 0.94, inner 0.93, upper 0.90, lower 0.76 tangents (the headset declares 1.79 / 1.02 / 1.22 / 1.02). The game's size scales each axis with the span of its two edges (0.67 wide, 0.74 high). The warp is drawn as a simplified two-zone model (1:1 centre, compressed periphery) fitted to the real 4224 × 1664 output; the centre size is a guess, and the real warp is smoother.
