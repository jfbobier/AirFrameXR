Frame size at each step, FOV restricted and foveated

One eye through the pipeline. The game renders a smaller picture, but every stage after composition carries the headset's full frame with a black margin where the game drew nothing.

Where the saving is

Only the game's GPU work drops: 6.4 Mpx per eye instead of 11.4, which is 56%. Encode time, bitrate and decode time stay where they were.

Why the margin is cheap

The margin lies in the periphery, where the warp shrinks the most. It is 44% of the composed frame but only 28% of the stream frame, and black blocks compress to almost nothing.

Headset side

The client unwarps into the headset's full field of view as before. The margin shows as black at the edges, with the picture placed inside it.

Illustrative numbers: a 3552 × 3200 per-eye headset frame (Vision Pro class), the game's recommended size equal to it, and the restriction used by the lab scenario e2e_fov_restrict (left eye: outer 70%, inner 90%, upper 80%, lower 60% of the tangents). The game size scales with each axis by the mean of its two edges (0.8 wide, 0.7 high). The warp is drawn as a simplified two-zone model (1:1 centre, compressed periphery) fitted to the real 4224 × 1664 output; the centre size is a guess, and the real warp is smoother.
